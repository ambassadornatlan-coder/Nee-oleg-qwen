Abstract

This report presents a rigorous, multi-agent scientific investigation into the state estimation of dynamic systems characterized by Markovian structural switching and discontinuous disturbances, with a specific focus on aircraft navigation applications. Recognizing the limitations of a single AI model executing concurrent physical agents, this research was conducted via three distinct, isolated analytical passes (Alpha: Mathematical Foundations, Beta: Algorithmic Estimation, Gamma: Aerospace Application) to simulate independent research trajectories. The investigation reveals that while the theory of generalized functions (Schwartz distributions) and semimartingales provides an elegant and mathematically rigorous framework for modeling discontinuous disturbances—naturally leading to jump-diffusion stochastic differential equations—the resulting exact filtering equations (e.g., the Kushner-Stratonovich equation for jump diffusions) are computationally intractable for real-time, Size-Weight-and-Power (SWaP) constrained avionics. The primary research gap identified is the lack of computationally tractable, finite-dimensional approximations of these exact measure-driven filtering equations that preserve the physical fidelity of the generalized function framework. The report concludes by recommending the development of adaptive mixture-reduction particle filters and variational approximations specifically tailored for aerospace kinematics to bridge this gap.

1. Introduction and Problem Formulation

The estimation of the state of dynamic systems in the presence of structural changes and abrupt disturbances is a fundamental problem in control theory and signal processing. In the context of aircraft navigation, this problem is exacerbated by the harsh physical environment and the critical safety requirements of the domain. Aircraft navigation systems, typically relying on the integration of Inertial Navigation Systems (INS) and Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS/GPS), are subject to two primary classes of anomalies:

  • Markov Switching: Discrete changes in the system's dynamic structure or parameters. In aircraft navigation, this corresponds to changes in flight regimes (e.g., transition from cruise to aggressive maneuvering), deployment of high-lift devices (flaps/slats) altering aerodynamic coefficients, or sudden sensor degradation.
  • Discontinuous Disturbances: Abrupt, non-smooth inputs to the system. These include severe atmospheric turbulence (microbursts), sudden control surface failures, or discontinuous measurement errors such as GPS multipath, cycle slips, and spoofing attacks.

Traditional approaches often model these phenomena using standard stochastic differential equations (SDEs) driven by Wiener processes, treating discontinuities either as outliers to be rejected or as high-variance process noise. However, this approach lacks mathematical rigor when the disturbances are truly discontinuous. The use of generalized functions (distribution theory) provides a rigorous mathematical foundation for modeling such disturbances, mapping them to Dirac delta functions or, in the stochastic case, to Poisson random measures. This leads to a formulation based on measure-driven SDEs or jump-diffusion processes.

The central scientific problem addressed in this report is: How can the mathematical rigor of generalized functions be leveraged to formulate state estimation algorithms for systems with Markov switching and discontinuous disturbances, and how can these theoretically exact algorithms be approximated to meet the strict real-time computational constraints of aircraft navigation systems?

3. Analytical Pass Alpha: Mathematical Foundations and Generalized Functions

Focus: Rigorous formulation of the dynamic system using distribution theory and stochastic calculus.

3.1. The Inadequacy of Classical Functions

In classical ordinary differential equations (ODEs), the state derivative \(\dot{x}(t)\) must be a standard function. If a system experiences a discontinuous disturbance (e.g., an instantaneous impulse), the classical derivative does not exist at the point of discontinuity. Modeling this by simply adding a large, narrow pulse to the input is mathematically inconsistent and leads to numerical instability in simulations.

3.2. Generalized Functions and Measure-Driven Dynamics

The theory of generalized functions, pioneered by Laurent Schwartz, allows us to extend the concept of a function to include distributions such as the Dirac delta \(\delta(t)\). A discontinuous disturbance \(d(t)\) acting at time \(t_k\) with magnitude \(a_k\) can be rigorously written as:

\[ d(t) = \sum_{k} a_k \delta(t - t_k) \]

When incorporated into a dynamic system \(\dot{x}(t) = f(x(t)) + d(t)\), the solution is no longer an absolutely continuous function, but a function of bounded variation. The state \(x(t)\) experiences a jump at \(t_k\): \(x(t_k^+) = x(t_k^-) + a_k\).

In a stochastic environment, the disturbance is not deterministic. The rigorous mathematical framework for stochastic systems with discontinuous disturbances is the theory of semimartingales and random measures (Jacod & Shiryaev, 2003). A continuous-time Markov switching system with discontinuous disturbances is formulated as a Jump-Diffusion process:

\[ dx_t = f(x_t, r_t)dt + g(x_t, r_t)dW_t + \int_{\mathcal{Z}} J(x_{t^-}, r_t, z) \mu(dt, dz) \]

Where:

  • \(r_t\) is a continuous-time Markov chain representing the structural mode.
  • \(W_t\) is a standard Wiener process (continuous Gaussian noise).
  • \(\mu(dt, dz)\) is a Poisson random measure representing the discontinuous disturbances (the generalized function framework applied to stochastic processes).
  • \(J(\cdot)\) is the jump amplitude function.

3.3. The Exact Filtering Equations

The use of generalized functions to model the disturbances directly dictates the form of the optimal Bayesian filtering equations. For a jump-diffusion process, the evolution of the conditional probability density \(p(x_t, r_t | Y_{0:t})\) (where \(Y\) is the observation history) is governed by the Kushner-Stratonovich equation for jump diffusions. Unlike the standard Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov equation which is a partial differential equation (PDE), the equation for jump diffusions is an integro-differential equation:

\[ \frac{\partial p}{\partial t} = \mathcal{L}^* p + \int_{\mathcal{Z}} \left[ \frac{p(x - J(z)) \lambda(z)}{p_{pred}} - p(x) \right] dz + \text{Measurement Update Terms} \]

Where \(\mathcal{L}^*\) is the adjoint of the diffusion generator, and \(\lambda(z)\) is the Lévy measure (intensity) of the jumps. The integral term explicitly accounts for the probability mass transferred by the discontinuous disturbances.

Pass Alpha Conclusion: The mathematically rigorous formulation using generalized functions inevitably leads to integro-differential filtering equations. While theoretically exact, these equations are infinite-dimensional and computationally intractable for real-time implementation. The mathematical elegance of the generalized function framework creates a severe computational bottleneck.

4. Analytical Pass Beta: Algorithmic Estimation and Computational Tractability

Focus: Translating the exact mathematical models into practical, computationally feasible estimation algorithms.

4.1. The Challenge of Non-Gaussianity

The integro-differential equations derived in Pass Alpha demonstrate that discontinuous disturbances destroy the Gaussianity of the state distribution. Even if the initial state and process noise are Gaussian, the jumps create a multimodal, highly non-Gaussian posterior distribution. The standard Kalman Filter (KF) and its extensions for Markov switching (like the Interacting Multiple Model - IMM) assume Gaussian distributions and are fundamentally inadequate for highly discontinuous disturbances unless the jumps are treated merely as increased process noise, which degrades estimation accuracy.

4.2. Interacting Multiple Model (IMM) and its Limitations

The IMM algorithm (Blom & Bar-Shalom, 1988) is the industry standard for Markov switching systems. It runs a bank of filters (usually KFs or Extended KFs) in parallel, one for each Markov mode, and mixes their estimates based on mode transition probabilities.

However, when discontinuous disturbances are present, the IMM faces a critical limitation: it assumes that within each mode, the noise is Gaussian. If a discontinuous disturbance occurs, it is either (a) absorbed into the process noise covariance (causing a temporary loss of tracking accuracy), or (b) modeled as a transition to a specific "fault" or "maneuver" mode. The latter requires an exhaustive enumeration of all possible jump magnitudes, which is computationally impossible for continuous-valued discontinuous disturbances.

4.3. Particle Filters and Gaussian Sum Filters

To handle the non-Gaussian posteriors induced by jump-diffusion processes, sequential Monte Carlo methods (Particle Filters - PF) and Gaussian Sum Filters (GSF) are required.

  • Particle Filters: PFs represent the posterior distribution using a set of weighted random samples (particles). They can naturally handle the integro-differential equations by simply applying the jump function \(J(\cdot)\) to the particles when a jump is detected or sampled. However, standard PFs suffer from particle depletion in the discrete Markov mode space. Specialized "Rao-Blackwellized" Particle Filters are required, where the continuous state is estimated by KFs conditioned on the discrete modes sampled by the particles (Doucet et al., 2000).
  • Gaussian Sum Filters: GSFs approximate the posterior as a weighted sum of Gaussian components. When a discontinuous disturbance occurs, new Gaussian components are spawned to represent the possible post-jump states. The critical challenge is mixture reduction: preventing the exponential growth of Gaussian components, which requires merging similar components and discarding those with low weights.
Pass Beta Conclusion: Exact Bayesian filtering for systems with Markov switching and discontinuous disturbances requires Particle Filters or Gaussian Sum Filters. The primary algorithmic challenge is not the handling of the jumps themselves, but the mixture reduction and the mitigation of particle depletion in the discrete mode space. Furthermore, these algorithms are highly computationally intensive, posing a severe challenge for embedded avionics.

5. Analytical Pass Gamma: Aircraft Navigation Context and Physical Disturbances

Focus: Grounding the mathematical and algorithmic theories in the physical realities and constraints of aircraft navigation.

5.1. Taxonomy of Discontinuous Disturbances in Aviation

In aircraft navigation, "discontinuous disturbances" are not abstract mathematical constructs; they represent specific physical phenomena that must be categorized to be modeled correctly:

  1. Process Discontinuities (Kinematic): Sudden changes in aircraft acceleration or angular rates. Causes include severe turbulence (microbursts), wake vortex encounters, or abrupt pilot inputs. These affect the INS mechanization equations.
  2. Measurement Discontinuities (Sensor Faults): Sudden jumps in sensor readings. Causes include GPS multipath (signals bouncing off buildings/terrain), GPS cycle slips in carrier-phase tracking, or sensor spoofing. These affect the measurement update equations.
  3. Structural Discontinuities (Markov Switches): Changes in the aircraft's physical configuration. Causes include landing gear deployment, flap extension, or control surface failures (e.g., a jammed rudder). These change the aerodynamic model and the relationship between control inputs and state derivatives.

5.2. The Identifiability Problem

A critical issue in aircraft navigation is distinguishing between a true structural switch (Markov transition) and a high-magnitude discontinuous disturbance. For example, a sudden pitch-up could be caused by a severe microburst (process discontinuity) or a sudden elevator hardover (structural change/fault).

If the estimation algorithm relies on generalized functions to model both phenomena, it must solve an identifiability problem. The filter must determine whether the innovation (the difference between predicted and actual measurement) is due to a continuous noise spike, a discrete jump in the state, or a transition to a new Markov mode. In highly observable environments (e.g., when GPS is available), this is manageable. In GPS-denied environments (relying solely on INS), the observability of the Markov modes and jump times degrades rapidly, leading to filter divergence.

5.3. SWaP Constraints in Avionics

Aircraft navigation computers operate under strict Size, Weight, and Power (SWaP) constraints. The computationally heavy algorithms identified in Pass Beta (Rao-Blackwellized Particle Filters, adaptive Gaussian Sum Filters) are generally too demanding for real-time execution on standard flight management system (FMS) hardware, which often relies on older, radiation-hardened, low-power processors.

Therefore, any theoretical framework based on generalized functions must ultimately be distilled into a highly optimized, finite-dimensional approximation that can run at 100Hz or higher (the typical INS update rate) with deterministic execution time.

Pass Gamma Conclusion: The physical reality of aircraft navigation introduces a severe identifiability problem between structural switches and process discontinuities, especially in GPS-denied environments. Furthermore, the strict SWaP constraints of avionics hardware mean that theoretically optimal, high-dimensional filtering algorithms are practically useless unless they can be reduced to deterministic, low-complexity approximations.

6. Cross-Agent Synthesis and Critical Analysis

The synthesis of the three independent analytical passes reveals a fundamental tension at the core of this research topic.

6.1. The Core Conflict: Mathematical Rigor vs. Computational Reality

Pass Alpha established that using generalized functions to model discontinuous disturbances is mathematically rigorous and leads to exact jump-diffusion filtering equations. However, Pass Beta and Pass Gamma demonstrated that these exact equations are computationally intractable and violate the SWaP constraints of aircraft navigation systems.

Resolution of the Conflict: The value of the generalized function framework is not in providing the final computational algorithm, but in providing the exact mathematical prior. The integro-differential equations derived via distribution theory define the theoretical lower bound of estimation error (the Bayesian Cramér-Rao bound for jump diffusions). The research challenge is not to discard the generalized function framework, but to develop variational approximations or moment-matching techniques that project the infinite-dimensional exact solution onto a finite-dimensional manifold suitable for real-time avionics.

6.2. Reconciling the Identifiability Problem

Pass Gamma identified the difficulty in distinguishing Markov switches from discontinuous disturbances. Pass Beta's analysis of Rao-Blackwellized Particle Filters offers a potential solution. By treating the Markov modes as discrete variables sampled by the particles, and the continuous state (including potential jump magnitudes) as the continuous variables, the filter can naturally explore the joint posterior of (Mode, State, Jump Time).

However, this exacerbates the computational burden. A promising synthesis is to use the generalized function framework to derive analytical jump conditions (e.g., if a jump of type A occurs, the state must satisfy constraint B). These analytical constraints can be used to prune the particle space or reduce the Gaussian mixture components, thereby mitigating the computational load while preserving the physical fidelity of the model.

7. Research Gap Assessment

Based on the synthesized findings, the following research gaps have been identified. They are ranked according to scientific importance, credibility, and potential contribution to the field of aircraft navigation.

Gap 1: Computationally Tractable Approximations of Jump-Diffusion Filters for SWaP-Constrained Avionics (Highest Priority)

  • Unresolved Question: How can the exact, infinite-dimensional Kushner-Stratonovich equation for jump-diffusion processes be approximated by a finite-dimensional, deterministic-time algorithm that meets the strict SWaP constraints of real-time aircraft navigation computers?
  • Existing Literature: Particle filters and Gaussian sum filters handle the non-Gaussianity (Doucet et al., 2000), but they suffer from variable execution times and high computational complexity. IMM handles Markov switching but fails on non-Gaussian jumps (Blom & Bar-Shalom, 1988).
  • Why it Matters: Without solving this gap, the mathematically rigorous framework of generalized functions remains a theoretical curiosity, unusable in actual flight-critical systems.
  • Required Work: Development of novel variational inference methods or deterministic moment-matching techniques specifically tailored to the kinematic constraints of 6-DOF aircraft motion.

Gap 2: Unified Measure-Driven Formulation for Simultaneous Process and Measurement Jumps

  • Unresolved Question: Can a single, unified mathematical framework based on generalized functions be formulated to simultaneously estimate discontinuous process disturbances (e.g., microbursts) and discontinuous measurement disturbances (e.g., GPS spoofing), while accounting for their distinct physical natures?
  • Existing Literature: Most literature treats process jumps (maneuvers) and measurement jumps (outliers/faults) using separate mechanisms (e.g., different noise covariances or separate FDI logic).
  • Why it Matters: In GPS-denied or degraded environments, the filter must rely on the INS. If a sudden aerodynamic disturbance (process) is misclassified as a sensor fault (measurement), the filter will incorrectly reject valid inertial data, leading to catastrophic drift.
  • Required Work: Formulation of a joint Poisson random measure for both process and measurement spaces, and derivation of the corresponding coupled integro-differential filtering equations.

Gap 3: Resolving the Identifiability of Structural Switches vs. High-Magnitude Disturbances in Low-Observability Regimes

  • Unresolved Question: How can the estimation algorithm reliably distinguish between a true Markovian structural change (e.g., flap deployment) and a high-magnitude continuous/discontinuous disturbance (e.g., severe turbulence) when external aiding sensors (GPS) are unavailable?
  • Existing Literature: Identifiability in jump Markov systems is well-studied for linear systems, but the non-linear, highly coupled kinematics of aircraft in GPS-denied environments remain underexplored.
  • Why it Matters: Misidentifying a disturbance as a structural change (or vice versa) leads to incorrect model selection in the IMM bank or incorrect particle weighting in a PF, causing filter divergence.
  • Required Work: Rigorous observability analysis of jump-diffusion systems representing aircraft kinematics, and the development of physics-informed priors to constrain the mode-transition probabilities based on aerodynamic feasibility.

8. Recommendations for Future Research

Based on the identified gaps, the following research directions are recommended for immediate and medium-term investigation.

Recommendation 1: Development of Physics-Informed Variational Jump Filters

Rationale: To address Gap 1, future research should move away from brute-force Monte Carlo methods (standard Particle Filters) and instead develop variational approximations.

Proposed Approach: Formulate the filtering problem as a variational optimization problem where the goal is to find a finite-dimensional parametric distribution (e.g., a Gaussian mixture with a fixed, small number of components) that minimizes the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence from the exact, infinite-dimensional posterior derived via generalized functions. Crucially, the variational parameters should be constrained by the physical kinematics of the aircraft (e.g., maximum possible jerk, aerodynamic limits), which will naturally regularize the problem and ensure deterministic execution times.

Recommendation 2: Formulation of a Joint Semimartingale Framework for INS/GPS Integration

Rationale: To address Gap 2, the mathematical foundation needs to be unified.

Proposed Approach: Extend the measure-driven SDE formulation to include both the process noise and the measurement noise as components of a joint semimartingale. Derive the exact filtering equations for this joint system. Then, investigate conditions under which the joint integro-differential equation can be decoupled into a continuous-time prediction step (handling process jumps) and a discrete-time update step (handling measurement jumps) without losing the cross-correlation benefits of the joint formulation. This will provide a mathematically rigorous alternative to ad-hoc Fault Detection and Isolation (FDI) logic.

Recommendation 3: Observability Analysis of 6-DOF Aircraft Jump-Diffusion Models

Rationale: To address Gap 3, theoretical guarantees of identifiability are required before algorithmic solutions can be trusted.

Proposed Approach: Conduct a rigorous nonlinear observability analysis (e.g., using the Hermann-Krener algebraic rank condition extended to jump systems) of a 6-Degree-of-Freedom aircraft model where both the aerodynamic parameters (Markov modes) and the external forces (discontinuous disturbances) are subject to jumps. Determine the specific flight trajectories (e.g., persistent excitation requirements) that are necessary to ensure that structural switches and process disturbances are locally observable. This will directly inform the design of flight-test protocols for validating new estimation algorithms.
